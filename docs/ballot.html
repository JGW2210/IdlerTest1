Plate i

The map is the progress bar

Numbers rising are a weak progression signal; a map filling in is a strong one. So the map is not a menu with scenery — it is the primary view, and every other system reports to it. You begin owning one hex and standing in it.

Part i

What I found in the brief

The setting arc is the strongest thing here. Villager → landholder → ruler works because the unit of play changes as you grow: first you swing the pick, then you own the mine, then you tax the valley the mine sits in. Most idle games only scale numbers. This scales verbs. Four things need resolving before any of it can be built.

Tension

“Idle” and “in-depth” pull opposite ways

Idle rewards absence. Depth rewards attention. Every game in this space picks a specific reconciliation and it dictates the whole architecture. The one I would pick: one focused action at full rate, plus background slots at reduced rate — because the background slots are the civilisation layer. Your first hired apprentice is your second slot. Realm growth and throughput growth become the same mechanic in two costumes, rather than a strategy game bolted onto an RPG.

Tuning target: an attentive hour should be worth about 1.8× an idle hour. Enough to reward presence; not enough to punish a day at work.

Correction

Shared parent XP must add, not divide

If a one-handed parent skill splits XP with its children, every weapon swap feels like a tax and players lock onto one weapon by hour two. Instead the child takes 100% and the parent takes an additional 30% — created, not divided.

Then make the parent gate the child: a child may not exceed parent + 10. Now the cheapest route to pushing your favourite weapon higher runs through its siblings, and breadth becomes a strategy instead of a chore. Parent levels grant accuracy, stamina economy and shared Mastery Points; child levels grant scaling, crit behaviour and that weapon’s techniques.

Warning

Count the bars before you commit

At the granularity the brief implies — sword, sabre, axe, mace, dagger, rapier and so on — five combat parents land at roughly 30 combat skills before a single pickaxe is swung. Total north of 45. RuneScape ships 23.

Forty-five bars reads as homework, and every one needs content at every tier or it becomes a dead limb. My counter: three children per parent, with weapon variants living inside a child. A sabre and an arming sword are both Blade — different speed, reach and scaling, no extra bar. Twenty combat skills that are all alive beats thirty where nine are abandoned.

Opportunity

The setting hands you a perfect reset

Idle games need a loop longer than “reach the next tier”, but maps and realms resist being wiped. Feudalism solves this for free: succession. Your character ages, retires or falls, and an heir inherits a fraction of what was built plus permanent bloodline traits earned from what the parent actually did. The realm, the map and your knowledge persist; the person resets.

It is a prestige system that never breaks the fiction — the only genre where the reset button is also the story.

Part ii

Runes should be a grammar, not a shop

“Elemental runes” plus “magical configurations in the loadout” is the seed of the best differentiator in this brief — provided magic is something you compose rather than something you buy. A spell is a sentence: an element, a form it takes, modifiers, and a trigger that fires it. Cost and effect are computed from the parts, so forty glyphs generate thousands of legitimate spells and the limiting factor is your cleverness, not the drop table.

Glyphs are found — in ruins, dungeon depths, distant tutors, and paid experimentation. Finished spells are inscribed onto Rune Tablets, which are equipment. Try it:

Part iii

Twelve tiers, and the problem underneath them

A pure ladder has a hidden cost: tier 9 deletes tiers 1 through 8, and with them the regions, recipes and skills attached to those tiers. Roughly two thirds of everything you build dies on contact with the next rung.

The fix is two extra axes. Alloys — your “special steel variants”, promoted from flavour to a real system — give co-equal choices within a tier rather than above it. Quality, from Crude to Legendary, means a Masterwork Steel blade can out-cut a Crude Mithral one, so old materials stay worth mining. Power climbs at 1.75× per tier while cost climbs at 2.30× — each rung is a real jump that takes visibly longer to reach.
